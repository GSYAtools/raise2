H5 post-reference lessons

1. The H5 fault tree contains numerous observation, representation,
communication, localization, computation, and control failures, but these do
not automatically establish additional phenomenon-level distinctions.

2. False positives, false negatives, missing, erroneous, stale, or delayed
information are classified as preservation/dependency threats unless the
downstream evidence establishes a distinct state of the declared Phenomenon
of Interest.

3. Omitted, erroneous, or delayed control/steering commands are execution or
control failures. They may cause concern-relevant phenomenon states but are
not themselves CRDs unless those command states belong to the declared PoI.

4. Timing relevance does not imply a temporal CRD. A temporal contrast is
admissible only when the timed property belongs to the declared PoI and its
collapse removes information required to evaluate C.

5. H5-CRD2 (within versus outside road boundaries) is directly aligned with
the top-level hazardous outcome and is supported by multiple downstream
causal branches.

6. H5-CRD1 (trajectory-consistent versus trajectory-inconsistent motion)
remains source-supported by the upstream functional requirements even when
the fault tree does not express it as an independent downstream distinction.

7. H5 does not provide evidence requiring decomposition of one evaluative
intent into multiple Phenomena of Interest. The development issue identified
in H2 is better addressed by enforcing PoI membership during CRD admissibility.
