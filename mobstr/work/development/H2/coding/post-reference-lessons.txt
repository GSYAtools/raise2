H2 post-reference lessons

1. Phenomenon-scope consistency
Every CRD must contrast situations belonging to the declared Phenomenon of
Interest. H2 exposed a potential inconsistency: lane-change initiation timing
and steering-command timing concern system-response behaviour, whereas the
initial H2 PoI was defined as the traffic/road situation determining necessity
and safe feasibility.

2. Possible PoI decomposition
When one evaluative concern depends on operationally distinct phenomena
(e.g., environmental situation and system-response timing), the method may
need either an explicitly composite PoI or multiple PoIs. This issue remains
provisional until tested against H5.

3. Abstraction
The FTA contains multiple technical failure mechanisms affecting lane,
localization, object, distance, and observation information. These do not
require separate CRDs when they threaten the same concern-relative
preservation obligation. This supports CP7.

4. Phenomenon/representation separation
False positives, false negatives, missing information, stale information,
and erroneous sensor or processing outputs are preservation threats rather
than CRDs unless they establish a source-supported difference in the PoI.
This supports CP6 and CP11.

5. Downstream non-invention
The presence of downstream failures such as wrong object classification does
not justify introducing an object-category CRD when the visible evaluative
source does not require such categories. This supports CP3.

6. Temporal-scope refinement
A temporal requirement is admissible as a CRD only when the timed event
belongs to the declared PoI (or to an explicitly identified additional PoI).
Temporal relevance alone is insufficient.
