RAISE2 REPRESENTATION REQUIREMENTS PROTOCOL
===========================================

PURPOSE

Apply the Representation Requirements activity of RAISE2 to the engineering
artefacts provided for a single engineering concern.

The objective is to derive the representational obligations that an Observable
Representation must satisfy for the concern to be operationally evaluated.

Use only the engineering artefacts provided in the experiment input. Do not
assume access to additional requirements, runtime telemetry, downstream
analyses, reference results, or information from previous executions.


GENERAL PRINCIPLES

1. Derive representational requirements from the engineering concern rather
   than from the information that happens to be operationally available.

2. Keep the Phenomenon of Interest distinct from sensors, telemetry, runtime
   models, algorithms, components, and other mechanisms used to observe,
   represent, process, or control it.

3. A requirement, architectural element, variable, failure mode, or other
   engineering element does not automatically define a Concern-Relevant
   Distinction.

4. Retain a distinction only when both contrasted situations belong to the
   declared Phenomenon of Interest and making them indistinguishable would
   remove information required to evaluate the Evaluation Target.

5. Do not attempt to construct an informationally complete representation.
   Preserve only distinctions established as necessary for the current
   Evaluation Target.

6. Do not invent thresholds, categories, assumptions, operational boundaries,
   or dependencies that are not supported by the provided engineering
   artefacts.

7. Preserve explicit traceability from every derived element to the source
   artefacts supporting it.


RR1 -- IDENTIFY THE EVALUATION TARGET

Identify the Evaluation Target T specified by the engineering artefacts.

T states the property, condition, behaviour, or relationship whose operational
state must be evaluated for the engineering concern.

Record:

- a unique identifier;
- a concise statement of T;
- the source artefacts supporting T;
- the engineering rationale.

Do not describe how T will be monitored or represented.


RR2 -- IDENTIFY THE PHENOMENON OF INTEREST

Identify the Phenomenon of Interest P that must be characterised to evaluate T.

P describes the system-related property, condition, behaviour, or relationship
about which the evaluation is made.

Define its operational scope sufficiently to determine which situations belong
to the phenomenon.

Record:

- a unique identifier;
- a concise statement of P;
- its operational scope;
- the source artefacts supporting P;
- the engineering rationale.

Do not define P in terms of a particular sensor, metric, log, data structure,
runtime model, or representation technology unless that mechanism is itself
the phenomenon being evaluated.


RR3 -- ELICIT CONCERN-RELEVANT DISTINCTIONS

Identify operational dimensions of P whose variation may alter the evaluation
of T.

For each candidate dimension, construct contrasting operational situations or
classes of situations and apply the collapse test:

"If an Observable Representation made these situations indistinguishable,
would information required to evaluate T be lost?"

Retain the candidate as a Concern-Relevant Distinction only when:

1. both contrasted situations are states within the declared scope of P; and
2. collapsing them would remove information required to evaluate T.

For every retained distinction record:

- a unique identifier;
- the evaluation dimension;
- contrast A;
- contrast B;
- the source artefacts supporting the distinction;
- the rationale explaining why the collapse test is satisfied.

Distinctions may describe classes or predicates rather than individual
enumerated situations.

Prefer the most general distinction supported by the engineering artefacts
when several variables or mechanisms impose the same preservation obligation.

Keep distinctions separate when they support different evaluative judgements
or require different distinguishability criteria.

Do not retain a candidate merely because it appears in a requirement or is
important to system operation.


RR4 -- SPECIFY OPERATIONAL REPRESENTATION REQUIREMENTS

Translate the retained Concern-Relevant Distinctions into Operational
Representation Requirements.

Each ORR must state what an admissible Observable Representation must preserve
without prescribing a particular sensor, metric, monitoring mechanism, data
source, model, or representation technology.

For every ORR record:

- a unique identifier;
- the Concern-Relevant Distinction or distinctions it covers;
- the preservation obligation;
- an explicit distinguishability criterion.

The distinguishability criterion specifies how preservation of the covered
distinction can be assessed. It may use categories, separation between values,
structural properties, or another criterion justified by the concern. Do not
invent a numerical threshold when the engineering artefacts do not provide or
justify one.

For each ORR, identify any assumptions on which its applicability or
satisfaction depends.

For every assumption record:

- a unique identifier;
- the assumption statement;
- the source artefacts supporting it;
- one or more revalidation conditions when applicable.

A revalidation condition identifies a class of lifecycle changes under which
the corresponding assumption, or its role in ORR satisfaction, must be
reassessed. It does not state that the assumption has already become false.

Do not invent assumptions solely to populate the output. If no explicit or
traceably supported assumption is identified for an ORR, return an empty
assumption list.


RR5 -- CHECK COVERAGE AND CONSISTENCY

Check the complete derivation before returning it.

Coverage requires every retained Concern-Relevant Distinction to be covered by
at least one ORR.

Consistency requires checking that:

- every ORR refers to an existing retained distinction;
- every retained distinction is covered;
- duplicate preservation obligations have not been introduced;
- preservation obligations are mutually compatible;
- each ORR has an assessable distinguishability criterion;
- every recorded assumption is traceable to provided engineering artefacts;
- every recorded revalidation condition is associated with an assumption.

Record whether coverage and consistency hold and report any identified issues.

Do not remove or silently repair an issue merely to make RR5 pass. Report the
issue in the structured output.


OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS

Return only one structured derivation conforming to the provided
derivation.schema.json contract.

Do not include explanatory text outside the structured output.

Use only source identifiers that occur in the supplied experiment artefacts.

Do not cite or infer information from artefacts that were not supplied.

Do not use knowledge from previous executions.

Treat each execution as independent.
